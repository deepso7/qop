title: How it works
description: Accounts, devices, the identity registry, and how qop delivers a message without a message server.
---

qop has four parts. Only one of them, the API, is a server that qop runs, and it never sees your messages.

| Part | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Identity registry** | A smart contract that maps each handle to an owner address and a list of device keys. It's the single source of truth for who owns which handle. |
| **API** | Pays the blockchain fees for registration and device changes. It checks your signatures and submits the transaction, but can't approve anything without them. |
| **App and CLI** | Create keys on your device, look people up in the registry, and talk to other devices over minip2p. |
| **minip2p** | The peer-to-peer transport. Devices connect directly, or through a relay when a direct path isn't possible. |

## Accounts and devices

An **account** is a permanent handle plus a numeric account ID (the _qid_). Two kinds of keys belong to it:

- **Owner key.** Controls the account: it approves adding and removing devices. You can export it as your [recovery key](/docs/devices#your-recovery-key).
- **Device keys.** Each device (a phone, a CLI) has its own key pair. The public half is recorded in the registry; the private half never leaves the device.

An account can have up to four active devices at a time. Owning the account and being able to send messages are separate: a device key lets a device message as you, but only the owner key can change the device list.

## The registry

The registry is a public contract on Ethereum (qop currently runs on the Sepolia test network). It enforces the rules for every account:

- Handles are 1–32 characters: lowercase letters, digits, and underscores, starting with a letter or digit. They're permanent.
- Every change to an account is signed by its owner key.
- A device key belongs to one account at a time. A removed key can't be added back; a device that's relinked gets a fresh key.

Anyone can read the registry, and records on it can't be deleted. See the [privacy policy](/privacy#public-registry) for exactly what becomes public.

Registration is invitation-only for now. The registry can be opened to everyone later, and once opened it can't be closed again.

## Sending a message

<Steps>
  <Step title="Look up the handle">
    The app reads the registry over a blockchain RPC provider to find the
    recipient's account and active device keys.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect">
    It opens a minip2p connection to one of those devices, directly or through a
    relay. The connection is encrypted in transit.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify">
    Both sides check the other's device key against the registry before any chat
    message is accepted. The check is cached for at most 60 seconds, cleared
    when the app resumes from the background, and redone on every new
    connection.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Deliver">
    The message is sent, stored on the recipient's device, and acknowledged.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Because there is no message server, the recipient's device has to be reachable. If you've [linked the CLI](/docs/cli), it can hold messages for your phone while it's offline.

## Terms

**Pairing** connects a new device to your phone so you can approve it. Pairing alone doesn't let the device send messages.

**Enrollment** adds the device's key to your account in the registry. From then on, it can send and receive as you.

**Revocation** removes a device's key. The device can no longer message as you, but conversations other devices already received stay where they are.

**Catch-up** is your phone reading messages a linked device saved while the phone was away, so they appear in the conversation when the phone returns.
